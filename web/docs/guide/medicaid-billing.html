NYS Medicaid billing for Collaborative Care

This page summarizes how a certified practice bills New York State Medicaid for Collaborative Care, based on OMH's billing guidance (updated June 2025, with changes effective September 1, 2025). It's included because billing and reporting draw on the same records, so a practice's billing lead and program lead need the same information.

This page is a summary and isn't billing advice. The rules and rates change, so check the current guidance with OMH at NYSCollaborativeCare@omh.ny.gov before submitting claims. This guide is an independent project and isn't published by OMH.

Who can bill

Practices certified in the NYS Collaborative Care Medicaid Program (about the program) can bill from the effective date OMH gives them. Article 28 clinics submit institutional claims; non-Article 28 practices (group practices and private practitioners) submit professional claims.

Which patients

Medicaid patients with an eligible diagnosis. Collaborative Care is carved out of Medicaid managed care, so claims for every Medicaid patient, managed care members included, go directly to fee-for-service Medicaid.

The list gives the recurrent mild depression code as "F33"; F33.0 is the billable code for it. Practices enrolling patients with ADHD must also report a Vanderbilt completion rate with the quarterly report.

Dual Medicare–Medicaid patients: Medicaid pays last. Bill Medicare first with the CPT codes below, then Medicaid, showing what Medicare paid. A month billed with T2022, which Medicare doesn't accept, can be zero-filled and sent straight to Medicaid.

What a billable month needs

For each patient, each calendar month:

  • at least one clinical contact, in which treatment is delivered and documented in the chart. It can be in person, a group session, or by phone if treatment is delivered;
  • at least one validated symptom-monitoring scale, recorded in the registry;
  • the patient managed in a state-approved registry;
  • the BHCM consulting a psychiatric consultant for an hour or more each week about enrolled patients. The consultant can't bill Medicaid for that consultation.

The first two requirements are the ones metric 5 counts. A month that meets both is a month of active treatment.

How to claim

  • Date of service: the last day of the month. If the patient's Medicaid eligibility ends during the month, use their last day of eligibility.
  • Article 28, institutional claims (837i or UB-04):
    • value code 24 followed by the rate code, for example 245246;
    • rate code 5246, the monthly case payment, with the procedure code on the line;
    • for the add-on, a second, separate claim with rate code 5261 and procedure code 99494;
    • rate codes 5247, 5248 and 5249 are no longer paid.
  • Non-Article 28, professional claims (837p or CMS-1500):
    • the procedure codes only, since professional claims carry no rate codes;
    • the directing primary care provider's NPI as attending provider, plus a Medicaid-enrolled referring provider if the attending isn't enrolled;
    • the billing provider needs specialty code 333 on their provider file, which OMH adds during certification;
    • Service Authorization Exception Code 7 (special handling), in box 25D of the eMedNY 150003 form.

Limits

  • 24 months of Collaborative Care per patient, not necessarily in a row. The limit resets after six months without a service. Most patients improve and are discharged within nine months.
  • Timely filing: 90 days from the date of service. Later claims need OMH's prior approval to use delay reason code 3, and must then be submitted within 30 days of the approval letter.
  • Earlier rules that no longer apply (from September 1, 2025): the 25% retainage, the 12-month limit, and the separate year-two rate.

Billing other services alongside

A practice can bill other services separately in the same month as the monthly case payment, but time spent on those services can't count toward the Collaborative Care minutes:

  • FQHCs: the PPS rate for face-to-face counseling by a psychiatrist, licensed psychologist or licensed social worker.
  • Article 28 clinics: a separate claim with the appropriate APG rate code for a psychiatrist's or licensed psychologist's services. A licensed social worker's face-to-face service to a pregnant woman or a child in the program uses special rate codes instead of APGs; the guidance lists 4222, 4223 and 4224.
  • Private practitioners: a separate professional claim for services within their scope.

Patients enrolled in a health home can also receive Collaborative Care.

How billing relates to reporting

  • Billed months aren't the same as enrolled months. A patient stays enrolled in a month with no billable contact; the guide counts enrollment from the registry, not from claims (what counts as enrolled).
  • Managed care members are Medicaid patients for both billing and reporting, which matches the guide's Medicaid call.
  • A billable month is a month of active treatment for metric 5: one clinical contact and one symptom scale.
