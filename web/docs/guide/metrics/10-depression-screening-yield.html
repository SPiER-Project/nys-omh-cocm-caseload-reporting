10. Depression screening yield

Number (#) and proportion (%) of all patients who scored a 10 or greater on their initial PHQ-9 during this month

— NYS, metric 10

Reported as: a count and a percentage. Target: none. The metric shows how much depression the screening finds; it isn't a performance measure. Population: the whole practice, all payers.

The rule

  • Denominator: patients with an initial PHQ-9 this month. That is a scored PHQ-9 (or PHQ-A) dated this month, with no other scored PHQ-9 in the 365 days before it. Each patient counts once, on their first PHQ-9 of the month.
  • Numerator: those whose initial PHQ-9 scored 10 or more. NYS says.
  • Report both: the numerator as the count, and numerator ÷ denominator as the percentage.

A PHQ-2 is never an initial PHQ-9. A CoCM patient's monthly monitoring PHQ-9 isn't either, because there's always an earlier one within a year. That keeps the metric focused on patients newly screened with the full instrument.

Worked example

From the screening example:

  • Patient B: initial PHQ-9 of 14, which is positive.
  • Patient H: initial PHQ-A of 7, which is negative.
  • Patient D: has monthly CoCM PHQ-9s, so their March PHQ-9 isn't initial.
  • Patient A: had only a PHQ-2.

Metric 10: 1 patient, 1 of 2, 50.0%.

Where the data lives

Use the same PHQ results as metric 9, with at least 13 months of PHQ-9 history, so that the 365 days before any PHQ-9 in the month are covered. See screening.

Common mistakes

MistakeEffect
Treating every PHQ-9 in the month as initialRepeat and monitoring scores dominate, and the yield is distorted
Counting PHQ-2 resultsWrong instrument; PHQ-2 has a different scale
Pulling too little history to see the prior 365 daysPHQ-9s are wrongly counted as initial
Reporting only the percentageNYS asks for the count too

For automation

For developers building their own report. Not needed to report.

The calculator reads scale_result (PHQ-9 and PHQ-A, with history back 365 days from the earliest PHQ-9 in the month).
