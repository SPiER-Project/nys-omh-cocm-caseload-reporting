The eleven metrics at a glance

What each metric counts, in a sentence, and how it's reported. Each one links to its own page, which has the full rule, a worked example, where the data lives and the common mistakes. Every rule is provisional until approved.

#MetricWhat it countsReported as
1Total enrollmentPatients enrolled in CoCM on any day this month, whatever their insuranceA count
2Medicaid enrollmentMetric 1, limited to patients with New York Medicaid on the first day of the monthA count
3Newly enrolledMedicaid patients whose initial assessment, and so their enrollment, was this monthA count
4Average duration of treatmentFor Medicaid patients discharged this month, the average time from enrollment to dischargeWeeks, to one decimal place. No value if nobody was discharged
5Monthly contact rateOf the Medicaid patients in metric 2, the share with both a clinical contact and a scored symptom scale this monthA percentage. Target: 80% or more
6Improvement rateOf Medicaid patients with 70 days or more in treatment and an elevated baseline, the share whose latest score on their primary scale meets NYS's improvement criteriaA percentage. Target: 60% or more
7Remission rateOf the Medicaid patients in metric 2, the share with an elevated baseline and a primary-scale score this month below the remission threshold (on the PHQ-9, below 5)A percentage. No target
8Psychiatric consultation rateOf the patients in metric 6's denominator who haven't improved, the share whose case the psychiatric consultant reviewed in the 60 days ending on the last day of the monthA percentage. Target: 80% or more
9Depression screening rateOf patients 12 or older with a qualifying medical visit this month, across the whole practice, the share with a PHQ-2 or PHQ-9 in the 12 months ending this monthA percentage. Target: 85%
10Depression screening yieldOf patients across the practice whose first PHQ-9 in 365 days was this month, those who scored 10 or moreA count and a percentage. No target
11Care manager staffing (BHCM FTE)The behavioral health care managers' time spent on CoCM this month, as full-time equivalentsA number such as 2.1, which the site attests

Who each metric covers

  • Metrics 1 to 8 are about the CoCM caseload. Metric 1 counts every payer; metrics 2 to 8 count Medicaid patients only.
  • Metrics 9 and 10 are about the whole practice, every payer, not only CoCM patients. Their data comes from billing and the EHR, not the CoCM registry.
  • Metric 11 is about staff, not patients.

How the rates fit together

  • Metrics 5 and 7 share a denominator: every Medicaid patient enrolled this month, which is metric 2.
  • Metric 8 picks up where metric 6 leaves off. Every patient in metric 6's denominator either improved, or goes on to metric 8's denominator.
  • Dividing metric 1 by metric 11 gives patients per care manager. NYS's optimal caseload is more than 75 per full-time care manager.

The terms these rules use, such as enrolled, elevated baseline and primary scale, are explained on the concept pages.
