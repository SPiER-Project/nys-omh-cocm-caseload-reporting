The NYS Collaborative Care Medicaid Program

Collaborative Care treats depression, anxiety and other common conditions in primary care. A behavioral health care manager leads each patient's care alongside their primary care provider, a psychiatric consultant reviews the caseload every week, and the team tracks everyone in a registry with regular symptom scales such as the PHQ-9.

New York's Office of Mental Health began paying for it through Medicaid in 2015, the first state to do so. More than 430 primary care practices now take part. Approved practices report caseload numbers to OMH each quarter: enrollment, contact, improvement and remission, psychiatric case review, and depression screening. This page counts them.

This calculator is an independent project, not an OMH product. Questions about the program go to OMH at NYSCollaborativeCare@omh.ny.gov. More about the program, and how Medicaid billing works.

  1. Gather your data

    The calculator reads eight tables: patients, coverage, CoCM enrollments, contacts, symptom scales, case reviews, practice visits, and a few program facts. Most sites fill in the workbook. Sites that pull reports from their systems use the CSV templates.

    • Reporting workbook for Excel, Google Sheets or Numbers (or the example filled in)
    • CSV templates, one file per table (or the example as CSVs)
    • Ready-to-send requests for billing, your EHR team and whoever keeps the registry

    Use each patient's MRN as their id in every file, so the records join.
